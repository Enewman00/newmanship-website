Here's a strange thing about the iPhone alarm: the volume buttons on the side of your phone might have nothing to do with it.

You can turn your music all the way up, watch a video at full blast, and still have an alarm that goes off at a polite murmur. It's one of the most common reasons people sleep through their alarm, and it's usually fixable in about two minutes.

## Your alarm uses a different volume

On iOS 26, the alarm in the Clock app doesn't follow your media volume. It follows the **Ringtone and Alerts** volume, the same one your ringtone uses.

To check it, open **Settings → Sounds & Haptics** and look at the Ringtone and Alerts slider. If it's sitting halfway, so is your alarm. Drag it to the right. Your phone will play a sample so you can hear how loud it is.

## The setting that keeps turning it down

Just below that slider is a toggle called **Change with Buttons**.

When it's on, the volume buttons on the side of your phone adjust your ringtone and alarm volume whenever you're not playing anything. That sounds handy, until you turn your phone down in a meeting at 3pm and forget about it. The alarm the next morning is just as quiet.

If you want your alarm to stay where you put it, switch Change with Buttons off. The side buttons will then only control media volume, and your alarm volume will stay wherever the slider is.

## Sleep schedule alarms have their own slider

If you set your wake-up time through a sleep schedule (in the Health app or the Sleep section of the Clock app) rather than as a normal alarm, it has its own volume control, separate from everything above. Open the wake-up alarm in your sleep schedule and check the slider there. A lot of people turn this one down once to avoid waking a partner and never turn it back up.

## Check the sound itself

This one's obvious once you see it: open the alarm in the Clock app and tap **Sound**. If it's set to *None*, you've got a vibration-only alarm. And some built-in tones are simply gentler than others. A soft chime is a lovely way to wake up if you're a light sleeper, and completely useless if you're not.

While you're there, try a few of the sharper tones and pick the one you'd least like to hear at 6am. That's probably the right one.

## Attention Aware Features

If your iPhone has Face ID, it can lower the volume of alerts when it detects you're looking at the screen. That's called Attention Aware Features, and you'll find it under **Settings → Accessibility → Face ID & Attention**.

It's unlikely to be the main reason you're sleeping through your alarm, since you're not usually looking at your phone when it goes off. But if you reach for your phone and the alarm suddenly drops in volume, this is why. Turning it off won't hurt.

## Silent mode and Focus

Good news here: the Clock app's alarms ring even when your phone is on Silent or in a Focus mode. Since iOS 26, third-party alarm apps built on Apple's AlarmKit can do the same. They're scheduled by the system itself, so they'll ring even if the app is closed. Older alarm apps relied on workarounds, so if you're using one that hasn't been updated in a while, it's worth checking.

## If it's loud and you still don't wake up

Sometimes the problem isn't volume at all. Some people, heavy sleepers especially, do hear the alarm, turn it off, and fall straight back asleep without any memory of it. Turning the volume up won't fix that, because the alarm isn't the weak point. The off switch is.

That's what wake-up missions are for: tasks you have to finish before the alarm will stop, like a math problem or finding something in another room.
